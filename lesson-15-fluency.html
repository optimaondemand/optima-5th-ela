This week you have been studying Johnny Tremain as a mentor text — noticing how Esther Forbes uses sensory detail to make 1773 Boston feel real. Today you will practice reading her opening passage with accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Great readers don’t just say the words — they bring a whole waking city to life with their voice.

This Week’s Fluency Passage
ON ROCKY ISLANDS gulls woke. Time to be about their business. Silently they floated in on the town, but when their icy eyes sighted the first dead fish, first bits of garbage about the ships and wharves, they began to scream and quarrel. The cocks in Boston back yards had long before cried the coming of day. Now the hens were also awake, scratching, clucking.
— Esther Forbes, Johnny Tremain, Chapter I
🗺️ Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
Read the passage again. Let the short sentences roll into one another instead of stopping hard after each one. Pause only where the commas and periods tell you to pause.
2
Read for Expression
Read it one more time. Start soft and watchful for “ON ROCKY ISLANDS gulls woke.” Let your voice rise and get sharper for “they began to scream and quarrel.” Then settle into a busy, matter-of-fact tone for the cocks and hens, like the town is simply getting on with its morning.
3
Record and Submit
When you feel ready, record yourself reading the passage with your best accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Upload your recording to Canvas.
✅ Fluency Self-Check
🎯 Did I say each word correctly, including sighted and wharves?
🌊 Did my reading sound smooth — the short sentences flowing together, not choppy?
🎭 Did my voice show the feeling — quiet gulls waking, then sudden screaming and quarreling, then a busy town starting its day?
📤 How to Submit

Record yourself reading the passage aloud. Upload your recording to Canvas using the assignment submission area. You’ve got this!
