furthermore praiseworthy's valor in chapter 10 demonstrates that true fortitude (–itude) isn't recklessness it is strength in service of what matters most — and the validity of his courage is proved by what he chooses to protect

Theme Analysis: Four Possible Claims
Choose the theme you can best support from the text
📌 Four Possible Theme Claims for By the Great Horn Spoon!

Theme A: Courage is not the absence of fear — it is acting well in spite of it.
Evidence in Chs. 1–12: Praiseworthy fears the Cape Horn stories but dismisses them publicly. He fears for Aunt Arabella’s house but stays composed. His punch in Ch.10 is the act of a man who is not reckless — but who acts when it matters most.

Theme B: Resourcefulness — the ability to find solutions with what you have — is more valuable than any map or treasure.
Evidence: Stowaway potatoes → grape cuttings saved. Beard hair → gold. Lumber → fuel for the race. Umbrella → gold pan. Each solution uses what was already there.

Theme C: Loyalty transforms people.
Evidence: Praiseworthy carries Arabella’s picture. Jack refuses to go home at Rio. Both become tougher, funnier, and more themselves because of their partnership. “Partners were the next best thing to being related.”

Theme D: Adventure doesn’t change who you are — it reveals who you already were.
Evidence: Praiseworthy was always dignified, resourceful, loyal. The gold rush didn’t make him that way. Jack was always brave and curious. The voyage didn’t create these qualities — it showed them.

💡

You can combine these themes, argue against them, or develop your own. What matters is that your claim is specific and your evidence is from the text.

📝 Theme Question 1
Which of the four theme claims above do you find most convincing — or what is your own theme claim? State it clearly in one sentence. Then explain in 2–3 sentences why you chose it and what the strongest piece of evidence is.
📓
📝 Theme Question 2
Find a second piece of evidence from Chapters 1–12 that supports your theme claim. Describe the event, quote or paraphrase the relevant moment, and explain specifically how it supports your claim. Use at least one transition word (therefore, furthermore, as a result, in contrast).
📓
📝 Theme Question 3 — Week Synthesis
Write your closing argument: restate your theme claim in different words, explain why it matters beyond the novel (what does it say about real people or real life?), and end with a sentence that captures why this story is worth reading. Use at least one abstract noun from this week (–ity, –ment, or –ness) and one transition.

💡

This week: shift to literary response. You’ve been writing your own adventure story. Now you write about Fleischman’s. A literary response makes a claim about theme — what Fleischman is saying about courage, resourcefulness, loyalty, or hope — and supports it with specific evidence from the text.

📋 Literary Response Structure

Opening: Name the book, author, and your theme claim.
"In By the Great Horn Spoon!, Sid Fleischman suggests that true courage is not the absence of fear — it is acting well in spite of it."

Body: Two or three pieces of evidence from the text, each explained. Use transitions: However, Furthermore, As a result, In contrast.

Closing: Restate what Fleischman is teaching — and why it matters.

Draft your literary response. Make a clear theme claim and support it with evidence.
