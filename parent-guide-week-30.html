This Week at a Glance

The novel ends this week. At Shirt-tail Camp, a crowd is about to punish Cut-Eye Higgins for stealing a horse, and Praiseworthy, who has never made a speech in his life, speaks up for him to keep a promise. The punishment he gets in return leads somewhere nobody expects. Then comes a day of celebration, the fight Praiseworthy promised to the Mountain Ox, a fast boat to San Francisco and one more surprise on the Long Wharf. On Day 4 your student looks back at the whole book: how the plot, the characters, the humor and the big ideas fit together.

💛 A note for families: words from 1963 and real history
This book was written in 1963. Two short passages this week use words from that time that are unkind, and a folded note on that day’s Reading tab names them plainly and gives the true history, with no made-up voices. Chapter 17 calls Chinese miners men “with pigtails” (a mocking word for the long braid, or queue, Chinese men were required to wear under the Qing rulers) and has the Mountain Ox order a cook named Ah Lee around at the “Chinese chow chow.” The note says that thousands of Chinese gold-seekers came in the early 1850s, worked old claims carefully, ran restaurants, laundries and stores, and faced unfair laws such as the 1852 tax on foreign miners. Chapter 18 mentions “Kanaka sailors”; the note explains that Kānaka is the Hawaiian word for people and that Hawaiian sailors worked on many Pacific ships. Chapter 16 has no slur, but its crowd means to hang a man with no lawyer and no judge; a real-history card on Day 1 says calmly that some mining camps really did hold quick, often unfair trials before California had state courts in 1850.
Reading skill
Putting it all together. Your student reads with four lenses at once (plot, character, humor and theme) and then looks back at the whole novel.
Big idea
Hope. When everything goes wrong, Praiseworthy looks at what he still has: “Mere bits of colored metal.”

New This Week

✉️ Jack’s Letter Home
The week’s word game. Each day there is a short, made-up letter from Jack with one misspelled word in every line. Your student taps the wrong word, picks the right spelling to ink the line clean, and seals the finished letter.
🌿 Virtue Watch: hope
After reading, your student sorts everyday examples of hope, picks a hopeful moment from the day of the fight, rates Praiseworthy’s hope after a big loss, and on Day 4 picks the most hopeful moment of the week.
🗺️ Real history, kept apart
A Day 1 card on the real Sutter’s Mill: James Marshall’s discovery on January 24, 1848, a worker’s diary from that day, and the Nisenan valley of Cullumah, plus the notes on 1963 words described above.
📖 Read Like a Writer: endings
Each day your student studies a real chapter ending, like “They hit bedrock. And struck gold.” and uses it to shape the ending of their own story.

This week your student finishes the adventure story they have built over the unit by giving it a strong ending. Day 1: write three notes: what happens at the very end, who the hero has become, and what the story means. Day 2: draft the conclusion paragraph, with sentence frames for help. Poetry Corner: How the Parts Combine

Gaily bedight,
A gallant knight,
In sunshine and in shadow,
Had journeyed long,
Singing a song,
In search of Eldorado.Edgar Allan Poe, “Eldorado” (1849)

Poe wrote this poem in 1849, the year the Gold Rush began. On Day 2 your student explains how two parts of the poem work together, like its rhyme and the word shadow that comes back in every stanza. On Day 4 they put all the parts together and say what the poem means about searching for gold.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did Praiseworthy speak up for Cut-Eye Higgins, a man who had cheated them?Listen for: he had given his word in a bargain for the map, and he kept it.
  2. How did the partners finally find gold?Listen for: they were made to dig a grave, and at four feet they hit bedrock and gold.
  3. Why did it matter so much when Praiseworthy called him “Jack”?Listen for: he had always said “Master Jack”; dropping it showed they were true partners.
  4. How did Praiseworthy beat the Mountain Ox?Listen for: by dodging, jabbing and following what he read in a book about boxing, not by being bigger.
  5. How did the book end? Were you surprised?Listen for: the gold was lost in the bay, the family came to California, and Praiseworthy proposed to Aunt Arabella.
  6. Tell me a word from the aud or val family.Listen for: audience, audible; valiant, valuable (or any real ones).
