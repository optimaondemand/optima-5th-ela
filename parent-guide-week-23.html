This Week at a Glance

Naomi is still hidden in the bonepickers’ wagon, and Leader is furious. In Chapter 24 he vows, “Someone will die today.” His soldiers shoot into the trees above the Choctaw Council, burn blankets at every camp, and reach Isaac’s family, where a rattlesnake stops a soldier from taking Nita’s body. At the bonepickers’ wagon, Leader orders the wagon burned with Naomi still inside, and Joseph leaps through the flames to pull her out. The week ends with Joseph and Naomi running for the river and Isaac seeing something frightening. In Writer’s Workshop, your student chooses their best writing from this unit and takes it all the way to a published piece.

💛 A heads-up for families
This is a tense week. In Chapter 24, Leader strikes one of his own soldiers with his shotgun, and the cut bleeds. In Chapter 25, falling branches knock an elderly woman unconscious. In Chapter 26, Nita’s mother shows the soldiers her daughter’s body. In Chapter 27, Leader throws an old woman to the ground and burns the wagon with Naomi hiding inside; she gets out, and the chapter ends on a frightening moment that is answered next week. The lessons name these moments calmly. Checking in after Chapters 26 and 27 can help.
Reading skill
Summarizing plot and theme. A summary is a choice: keep the events the story could not do without and leave the rest out. On Day 4, your student puts the book together so far: character, conflict, point of view and theme.
Big idea
Justice starts with refusing to look away. Mister Tibbi stands his ground, Ruth tells the truth without a word of argument, and the soldiers start to see the man they follow.

“We did nothing to deserve this.”Mister Tibbi, Chapter 25

Words to Know

Use them at home!
exhaustedcompletely worn out
revengehurting someone back for something they did to you
unconsciousnot awake and not able to feel anything, usually after being hurt
gratitudethankfulness, and the showing of it
prophecya prediction of what will happen in the future
Word parts
voc / voke means “voice, call,” and dict means “say.” An advocate raises a voice for someone; a verdict is the truth, spoken.vocal · advocate · provoke · evoke · predict · contradict · verdict · dictate
Spelling
A year of patterns, used on a real draft. Suffixes that change the base, prefixes that stay whole, sound-alikes, -able and -ible, and Greek and Latin parts.decision · illegal · their / there · dependable · visible · manuscript
Grammar
Editing for clarity and sentence combining. Short sentences are joined so the reader can see how ideas connect, and transition words (but, because, when, instead) show the link.It was freezing, but Joseph was so worn out that he fell asleep in minutes.

New This Week

📣 Raise Your Voice
The week’s word game. Each line has a blank, and your student taps the voc or dict word that fits (dictionary, vocation, unpredictable). Each finished line sends a gold voice ring into the sky. Three lines a day.
🌿 Virtue Watch: justice
After reading, your student sorts moments, keeps a log, rates a moment on a meter, and on Day 4 picks the strongest moment of justice in the week.
🖼️ Art from long ago
Each lesson opens with a work of art: Monet’s snowy morning, Friedrich’s oak and his ancient grave in the snow, and a hand-colored rattlesnake from a 1743 book about Carolina. They were made far from Mississippi, and the captions say so.
❓ Do you keep following him?
Day 2 opens with a question: if your leader hurt one of your own, would you keep following him? Chapter 25 shows what Leader’s soldiers start to wonder.

Your Student’s Writing: A Published Piece

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

Your student finishes the unit by publishing their best writing. Day 1: look back at the writing from weeks 18 to 22, choose the strongest piece (or combine the best parts), and plan what to keep, cut, and how to begin and end. Day 2: write a full draft. Day 3: revise with a checklist: strong verbs, a combined sentence, clear pronouns, and at least one transition word. Day 4: write the final version and one sentence about what it shows about them as a writer.

Every day also has a Read Like a Writer box with one of the book’s sentences, like “The soldiers continued their search, burning blankets and questioning Choctaws at every camp.” This week they show how one sentence can sum up a lot without retelling every event.

Poetry Corner: Why This Shape?

Under the wide and starry sky,
Dig the grave and let me lie.
Glad did I live and gladly die,
And I laid me down with a will.Robert Louis Stevenson, “Requiem” (1887), first stanza, Day 4. On Day 2: Emma Lazarus, “The New Colossus” (1883).

This week’s poems are about shape. Lazarus wrote a sonnet, a strict fourteen-line form, for the words the Statue of Liberty speaks. Stevenson shaped “Requiem” like a gravestone: two short, calm stanzas, with the words he wanted carved on the stone.

How to help: Read “Requiem” aloud together. Ask why a poem about the end of a life might be so short and calm.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What did Leader vow, and how did his own soldiers feel about him?Listen for: “Someone will die today.” After he struck Pointy Nose, they became afraid of him; by Chapter 27 they see him as a cruel man.
  2. Chapter 25 is called “A Day of Death.” Does anyone die in it?Listen for: no. Stella is hurt and blankets are burned, but Leader says no one died. A careful summary notices that.
  3. What did Ruth do when Leader demanded her little girl?Listen for: she did not argue. She laid Nita’s body by the fire and said, “Here is my daughter.” Then a rattlesnake guarded Nita.
  4. How did Naomi get out of the burning wagon?Listen for: Joseph leapt through the burning curtain, lifted the false floor of the trunk, and jumped out with her through the cloth wall.
  5. Sum up this week’s chapters in three sentences.Listen for: only the events the story could not do without, not every detail.
  6. Read me your published piece. What does it show about you as a writer?Listen for: a clean, finished piece and one honest sentence about their writing.
