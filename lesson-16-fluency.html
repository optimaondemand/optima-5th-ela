This week you have been researching the Choctaw Nation's homeland before 1830 — the rivers, fields, and communities that shaped daily life for generations. Today you will practice reading a passage from your own research with accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Good readers of nonfiction let their voice show why a place, and its history, matters.

This Week’s Fluency Passage
Before the 1830s, the Choctaw Nation’s homeland stretched across a large part of what is now the state of Mississippi. Choctaw families lived in settled communities, growing corn, beans, and squash in the fertile river-bottom fields of their territory. For generations, the Choctaw Nation’s identity was tied to this land — the rivers, fields, and forests that had supported their communities for centuries. By the early 1800s, this homeland covered millions of acres.
— This Week’s Reading, Day 1: “The Choctaw Homeland”
🗺️ Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
Read the passage again. This time, keep your voice flowing through the longer sentences without choppy stops, pausing only at the commas and periods.
2
Read for Expression
Read it one more time. Let your voice slow down and grow a little reverent on “the rivers, fields, and forests that had supported their communities for centuries.” Then let the short final sentence land clearly and simply: “this homeland covered millions of acres.”
3
Record and Submit
When you feel ready, record yourself reading the passage with your best accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Upload your recording to Canvas.
✅ Fluency Self-Check
🎯 Did I say each word correctly, including Choctaw and territory?
🌊 Did my reading sound smooth through the longer sentences — steady and flowing, not choppy?
🎭 Did my voice show the feeling of this passage — a sense of history, place, and generations of home?
