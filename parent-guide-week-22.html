This Week at a Glance

Isaac, now a ghost, reaches Naomi in the soldiers’ wagon, and she learns her little sister Nita has died. When Leader hears voices, he punishes Naomi. That night Isaac, Joseph, Nita’s ghost, a dog named Jumper and the Choctaw Council work together to free her: Jumper splashes into the river, Joseph as the panther gnaws through the rope and sets a wagon on fire, and Naomi runs. Luke takes her to the bonepickers, four old women who hide her in a trunk with a secret bottom. At the end of the week, a hundred Choctaw ghosts appear to thank the three boys. In Writer’s Workshop, your student writes a one-paragraph literary analysis.

💛 A heads-up for families
This is a hard week to read. In Chapter 20, Leader hangs Naomi from a tree by her tied wrists and slaps her. Chapter 22 explains, plainly, how the bonepickers cared for the dead in the old Choctaw way. In Chapter 23, the women wring a bloody raccoon over the bones in the trunk so the soldiers will not look inside, and the blood drips on Naomi. The lessons name these moments calmly and do not dwell on them. Reading Chapters 20 and 23 together, or checking in afterward, can help.
Reading skill
Figurative language and the author’s choices. Your student marks comparisons and images, like Nita shining “like a pale moon,” and asks what each one makes a reader feel.
Big idea
Strength can look quiet. Naomi protects her friends with two words, “No one,” and later lies still in the dark to stay hidden.

“I have to be watchful to stay alive.”Naomi, Chapter 20

Words to Know

Use them at home!
distractionsomething that pulls attention away, so something else can happen unnoticed
bonepickersChoctaws whose sacred job was to prepare the dead for burial
speechlessso surprised or moved that you cannot say a word
ancestorsthe family members who lived long before you
honoredtreated with great respect
Word parts
luc / lum (Latin) and photo (Greek) both mean “light.” Something translucent lets light pass through.lucid · translucent · illuminate · luminous · photograph · photosynthesis
Spelling
Greek and Latin roots in book words. Long words about stories are short parts stacked together.chron (time) · graph (write) · log (word) · phor/fer (carry): chronology · paragraph · dialogue · metaphor
Grammar
Pronoun clarity. A pronoun must point back to one clear noun. With Isaac, Joseph, Luke and Leader all called “he,” names matter.Isaac told Joseph that Joseph should go first.

Your Student’s Writing: A Literary Analysis Paragraph

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

Your student writes one paragraph that makes a claim about the novel and proves it. Day 1: choose a topic (Naomi’s strength, Isaac’s role as a ghost, or justice), write a claim, and find two pieces of evidence. Day 2: draft the paragraph: claim, two pieces of evidence with explanation, and a closing sentence. Day 3: revise with a checklist: clear pronouns, one better transition, no extra words. Day 4: write the final paragraph, then explain the difference between a summary and an analysis.

Every day also has a Read Like a Writer box with one of the book’s sentences, like “Fat balls of tears rolled down her cheeks.” This week they show how imagery and comparisons let a reader feel a moment.

🪶

Poetry Corner: Pictures Instead of Feelings

Whose woods these are I think I know.
His house is in the village though;
He will not see me stopping here
To watch his woods fill up with snow.Robert Frost, “Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening” (1923), first stanza, Day 4. On Day 2: William Blake, “The Tyger” (1794).

Neither poet tells you what to feel. Blake gives you a tiger “burning bright” in a dark forest. Frost gives you woods filling up with snow on the darkest evening of the year. That is imagery: pictures that carry the feeling.

How to help: Read the Frost poem aloud together. Ask which picture makes the woods feel quiet, and why he repeats the last line.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did Naomi say at first that she could not escape?Listen for: she thought the soldiers would take her little sister, Nita. Then she learned Nita had already died.
  2. Who helped Naomi get away, and what did each one do?Listen for: Jumper splashed into the river, the panther gnawed the rope and set a wagon on fire, Nita woke the council, a councilwoman cut the rope, Luke led her to the bonepickers.
  3. Who are the bonepickers, and why is their wagon a safe hiding place?Listen for: old Choctaw women whose sacred job was caring for the dead. Most people stay away from them, and the trunk has a secret bottom under the bones.
  4. What did the ghosts say to Isaac, Joseph and Luke?Listen for: they were proud of the boys and said “thank you.” Making the ancestors proud is the highest honor.
  5. Find a comparison in this week’s chapters. What does it make you feel?Listen for: an image like Nita shining like a pale moon, or Naomi’s realization “like a thunderbolt.”
  6. Read me your paragraph. What is your claim, and what proves it?Listen for: one clear claim, two pieces of evidence from the book, and an explanation of each.
