Sentence 1 — The Correct Model

Because his hands were still trembling from the cold and the fright of the night, Nathaniel pushed open the print shop's heavy door, and the warm, ink-scented air wrapped around him like relief.

Sentence 2 — Fix It

This sentence has mistakes. Rewrite it correctly in your notebook, using the model as a guide.

the printer looked up quickly since he had not expected a visitor before dawn nathaniel held out the letter with a shaking hand

Corrected:

The printer looked up quickly since he had not expected a visitor before dawn. Nathaniel held out the letter with a shaking hand.

Compare with the model: both sentences begin with a capital letter, and this was really two separate sentences joined incorrectly into a run-on — it needed a period and a capital letter to start the second sentence.

🔠 Spelling: Suffix Changes in Narrative Verbs
Apply: write your own examples

Remember the three rules for adding -ed or -ing to a verb: drop the silent e (hope → hoping), double the final consonant (stop → stopping), and change y to i before -ed only (worry → worried). Today, write your own example of each rule.

What Good Writers Do
Set your revision lens

Good writers know a first draft is never the final draft. Today is about looking at your own words with fresh eyes and asking what could be stronger — not starting over, just sharpening what's already there.

As you revise today, ask yourself:

🧠 Watch for this

Which part of my draft am I most proud of, and why?

Where could a stronger sensory detail or a more precise verb make a moment come alive?

Does my dialogue sound like something my character would really say out loud?

Ms. Birchum's Example
A revision, not a first draft
Compare the rough version to the revised version below.
📌 Before revision (rough draft)

“Nathaniel walked to the print shop and gave the letter to the printer.”

✨ After revision (stronger ideas, organization & voice)

“Because his hands were still trembling from the cold and the fright of the night, Nathaniel pushed open the print shop's heavy door, and the warm, ink-scented air wrapped around him like relief.”

The revised sentence adds a complex sentence to show cause and effect (ideas), keeps the moment in order right after the close call with the soldier (organization), and adds sensory detail and a simile that sound like a real writer's voice, not just a list of events (voice).

💡 Ideas

Does every sentence add a detail your reader needs, instead of just repeating what they already know?

🗂️ Organization

Do your events happen in an order that makes sense, with each moment building on the last?

🎤 Voice

Does it sound like a real storyteller wrote it — with sensory detail and precise verbs — instead of a plain list of events?

🔤️ Complex Sentence

Could you combine two short sentences using because, since, until, or although to show cause and effect?
