🎙️Week 29 · Fluency

This week Jack fell into a coyote hole with a grizzly nearby, an unloaded squirrel gun, and no one in earshot. The man who rescued him had some parting advice. Today you practice the most comic exit line in the novel — the road agent who is totally right and knows it.

📜 Week 29 Fluency Passage — Chapter 15: The Man Who Couldn’t Sit Down
The ex-highwayman started away and then turned with a final laugh.

“Boy, the next time you point that squirrel gun at a bad hombre like me, you really ought to trouble yourself to reload it first. Good luck, boy.”

Jack’s face reddened under the layers of dust. He watched the man disappear through the trees. He was sorry he hadn’t been more polite to his benefactor.

“Thank you, sir!” he called.
— Sid Fleischman, By the Great Horn Spoon!, Chapter 15
🏔️ Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
The road agent’s speech is one long sentence that builds — let it roll out naturally without rushing. Then Jack’s reaction should feel slower, more internal: “His face reddened under the layers of dust.”
2
Read for Expression
The road agent is cheerful and entirely right. Jack is embarrassed and a little ashamed of himself. The final “Thank you, sir!” — called after the man is already gone — should land as the perfect, too-late, too-polite ending. Give it the pause it deserves before Jack calls it.
