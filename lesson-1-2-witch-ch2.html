What Good Readers Do
Set your reading lens

Good readers analyze how setting launches conflict. As Kit moves closer to Connecticut, each new detail the author reveals deepens the tension between her old world and the one she is about to enter.

🔍 How to Do It
  1. Notice the details: What does the author describe about the place, the people, and the way of life?
  2. Compare worlds: How is this setting different from what the character is used to? What clashes?
  3. Predict conflict: Based on the setting details, what problems or tensions might arise for the character?

🌱 Morphology: Morpheme Launch
English & Latin origins · understanding word parts

A morpheme is the smallest unit of meaning in a word. When you can spot word parts, you can figure out unfamiliar words on your own. Every word has at least one morpheme, and many have more.

📌 Key Word Parts

dis- + approv(e) + -al = disapproval — the prefix dis- means "not," the base is approve, and -al makes it a noun

un- + comfort + -able = uncomfortable — the prefix un- means "not," and -able means "can be"

im- + patient + -ly = impatiently — im- means "not," and -ly makes it an adverb

respect + -able + -ly = respectably — -able means "can be," and -ly makes it an adverb

✏️ Grammar: Sentences, Fragments, & Run-Ons
Spot them in sentences from the novel
📖

Remember the Rule: A complete sentence has a subject (who or what), a predicate (what the subject does), and expresses a complete thought. A fragment is missing one of those pieces. A run-on jams two complete sentences together without proper punctuation.

👉 From the novel

“Kit watched the sailors from the deck.” — This is a complete sentence. It has a subject (Kit), a verb (watched), and expresses a complete thought.

🔠 Spelling: Syllable Types
Identify syllable types in novel words
📖

The Rule: Every syllable has one vowel sound. The six syllable types — closed, open, VCe, vowel team, r-controlled, and consonant-le — tell you whether the vowel is long or short. Knowing the type helps you decode unfamiliar words.

Yesterday you discovered the six syllable types. Today, look at each word from the novel and identify what syllable type it contains. Think about it, then tap to check!

📌 Quick Reminder

Closed = short vowel, closed by a consonant (cap, stan). Open = ends in a long vowel (me, go). VCe = silent e makes the vowel long (name). R-controlled = vowel + r changes sound (riv-er). Vowel team = two vowels together (boat). Consonant-le = consonant + le at the end (can-dle).
