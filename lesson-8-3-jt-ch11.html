Tap words you notice are correct. Watch for capitals, commas, and punctuation.

Sentence 2 — Fix It

This sentence has mistakes. Rewrite it correctly in your notebook, using the model as a guide.

the colonys of new england sent their best men to the first continental congress in philadelphia

Corrected:

The colonies of New England sent their best men to the First Continental Congress in Philadelphia.

Compare with the model: “Colonys”→“colonies” (Rule 3: y→ies). “New England” is a proper noun—both words capitalized. “First Continental Congress” is a proper name—all main words capitalized. “Philadelphia” is a proper noun—capital.

🌱 Morphology: Roots dict & scrib/script
Latin roots · dict = say/declare · scrib/script = write

Two Latin roots appear throughout academic and literary vocabulary. Dict means “say” or “declare.” Scrib and script mean “write.” Knowing these roots helps you decode unfamiliar words.

📌 Use These Prefixes

You’ve defined and identified sub- and inter-. Now use them in sentences about Johnny Tremain.

From Chapter 11: Earl Percy came to intervene when Colonel Smith’s troops were nearly overwhelmed. The colonists kept their powder stores submerged in secrecy. Even ordinary citizens tried to intercede by hiding rebel printers in feather beds.

sub- pushes things down or under: submerge, subdue, submit

inter- places things between: intercept, intervene, intercede

From Chapter 11: When Percy’s brigade came to intercede for Smith’s struggling troops, the colonists were no longer content to remain subordinate—they had moved from bystanders to fighters.

🌱

Practice: Prefix/Root Match! Tap each word, then tap its meaning.

subordinate
placed under someone else in rank (sub-)
subside
to settle or sink lower; to calm down (sub-)
intercede
to go between two parties to help (inter-)
intermediate
occurring between two points or stages (inter-)
interact
to act between people; to communicate (inter-)
🌱 All matched! You can use sub- and inter-.

🔠 Spelling: Possessives vs. Plurals
Apply · Sort into plural, singular possessive, plural possessive

Today: apply the rules by sorting words into three groups—plural, singular possessive, or plural possessive.

📌 Three Categories

Plural (more than one, no apostrophe): soldiers, drums, horses

Singular possessive (one owner): soldier’s, Johnny’s, Percy’s

Plural possessive (many owners): soldiers’, officers’, patriots’

From Chapter 11: The soldiers (plural) marched out with Percy’s (singular possessive) brigade, while the patriots’ (plural possessive) muskets waited in readiness.

🎮

Practice: Three-Way Sort! Sort each word into the correct column.

RWM — Read like a Reader, Read like a Writer, Mimic
“The drums throbbed. The heavy dragon marched on its thousands of feet, and now above the drums came the shrilling of the fifes.”
📖 Read like a Reader
Earl Percy’s brigade is marching out of Boston toward the battle. What does this description make you feel? Forbes calls the army a “heavy dragon.” What does that metaphor do to your sense of what Johnny is watching?
✏️ Read like a Writer
Three very different sentences. The first has just two words: subject + verb. The second is long, building momentum with the word “thousands.” The third introduces a sound you didn’t expect: the shrilling of the fifes. How does the sentence length change the rhythm? How does Forbes use sound to make the march feel real?
🎨 Mimic
The ___ ___ed. The heavy ___ moved on its ___, and above it came the ___ of the ___.
Write three sentences showing a large, powerful thing moving. Start with a short 2–4 word sentence. Then write a longer sentence with a metaphor or comparison. End with one surprising sound or detail. Build your sentences from shortest to most complex.

Yankee Doodle

Word reaches Boston that fighting has broken out at Lexington and Concord. Doctor Warren pulls Johnny aside, tells him what happened, and sends him out into the anxious, crowded streets to gather information. All day Johnny reads the mood of the city—watching soldiers, colonists, and rumors collide. By evening, Earl Percy’s brigade marches out to rescue Colonel Smith’s exhausted, retreating regiment, and Johnny watches the “heavy dragon” of the British army pass by, aching that his ruined hand keeps him from ever picking up a gun himself.
