This Week at a Glance

The stagecoach climbs into the gold country with Cut-Eye Higgins aboard. Road agents stop the stage, and Praiseworthy keeps their gold dust safe in the fingers of his glove, until a robber takes the one thing he will not give up. In Hangtown, Jack learns to pan from a miner named Pitch-pine Billy, earns the name Jamoka Jack, and Praiseworthy’s umbrella becomes a gold pan. Back in town, the partners find that the story of the robbery has grown. On Day 4 your student names a theme of the novel and argues for it with evidence.

💛 A note for families: real history this week
Chapters 10 to 12 do not use the 1963 language about any group of people that appeared in Chapters 8 and 9. The hard history this week is the name Hangtown. It was a real gold camp, first called Dry Diggings. In 1849 a crowd there hanged three men accused of robbery, without a real trial, and the camp was called Hangtown after that; in 1854 it became Placerville, the name it still has. The book jokes about the name, as many old stories did. A short folded card on Day 2 says what really happened, calmly, and keeps it apart from the novel. The same card notes that dry washing, which Pitch-pine Billy shows Jack, was a method of miners from Sonora, Mexico, and that in 1850 California taxed miners who were not American citizens $20 a month, a law that drove many Mexican and Latin American miners from the mines before it was ended in 1851. Sources: City of Placerville; California State Parks; Calisphere (University of California).
Reading skill
How events and conflicts build a theme. Your student collects events and conflicts, watches what each character chooses, and names the big idea they add up to.
Big idea
Honesty. Praiseworthy tries to tell Hangtown the truth, “But not under false colors,” and then has to decide what to do when no one wants to hear it.

Roots
fort means strong and val / valid mean strong or worth.fortify · comfort · valuable · evaluate
Spelling
-ity, -ment and -ness turn a word into the name of an idea. Watch the base: lonely → loneliness, argue → argument, curious → curiosity.boldness · achievement · validity
Grammar
Transitions and introductory phrases link ideas and take a comma.At first, Jack could barely swallow the coffee. However, he kept drinking.

New This Week

🪙 Pan for Color
The week’s word game. Each pan holds four words: two are gold and two are slickens (mud). Your student washes out the misspelled endings and the look-alike words with no real fort or val root (like fortune and valley), and the gold goes into Jack’s pouch.
🌿 Virtue Watch: honesty
After reading, your student sorts everyday examples, picks the most honest moment in Chapters 10 and 11, rates how honest Praiseworthy is with the tall tale, and on Day 4 picks the most honest moment of the week.
🗺️ Real history, kept apart
Two short cards on Day 2: the true story of Hangtown and the miners from Sonora, and the miners’ words in the book (jamoka, spangles, slickens, mountain canary), each with the line where it appears.
📖 Read Like a Writer, every day
One line from the book a day, chosen to show how a character’s choice builds a theme, like Praiseworthy’s warning about Aunt Arabella’s picture: “I’ll thank you to return that picture to my bag.”

Your Student’s Writing: A Literary Response

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

This week your student writes about the novel: a claim about its theme, proved with what characters choose to do. Day 1: plan a one-sentence theme claim and two character choices that could prove it. Day 2: draft, with sentence frames for help. Day 3: revise with a checklist: a claim that is an idea, not a topic; two pieces of evidence, each explained; a transition; and a closing that says why it matters. Day 4: submit.

Poetry Corner: More Than One Theme

He clasps the crag with crooked hands;
Close to the sun in lonely lands,
Ring’d with the azure world, he stands.Alfred, Lord Tennyson, “The Eagle” (1851)

Is this a poem about power or about being alone? Both are in the words. On Day 2 your student argues for both readings; on Day 4 they look for one scene in the novel that carries two themes at once.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did Praiseworthy keep the gold dust safe from the road agents?Listen for: it was in the fingertips of his glove; he pulled the glove off “carefully but casually,” and the dust stayed inside.
  2. What finally made calm Praiseworthy lose his temper?Listen for: a robber took Aunt Arabella’s picture.
  3. How did Jack earn the name Jamoka Jack?Listen for: he kept drinking Pitch-pine Billy’s terrible coffee so the miner wouldn’t take the name back.
  4. Why was the town called Hangtown?Listen for: a crowd hanged men there in 1849 without a real trial; later the town was renamed Placerville.
  5. What theme did you choose, and what is your best evidence?Listen for: an idea about life (not just a topic) and a character’s choice from the book.
  6. What do fort and val mean? Name a word for each.Listen for: strong (fortify, comfort); strong or worth (valor, valuable).
