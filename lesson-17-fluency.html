Johnny Tremain — One Last Time
🪶 Week 17 · Fluency

Before you spend this week building your own historical fiction story, take one last chance to hear Johnny Tremain’s voice out loud. This is a small, quiet moment near the end of the trial — no shouting, no big speeches, just a gesture that says more than words could. Next week we meet a new narrator and a new story. Today, read this one like you mean it.

This Week’s Fluency Passage
Then she tried to kiss Johnny, but he thought it beneath his dignity to be kissed on the street. “You’re too mussed up with licorice,” he said. She bent and kissed his burned hand. He said nothing. He was suddenly afraid he might cry.
— Esther Forbes, Johnny Tremain, Chapter IV, “The Rising Eye”
🗺️ Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
Read it again. Keep the dialogue and the narration flowing together naturally — don’t let the quotation marks make you stop and start choppily. Pause only where the commas and periods tell you to.
2
Read for Expression
Read it one more time. Johnny is trying to sound tough and unbothered — “You’re too mussed up with licorice” should sound almost joking. Then slow way down for the last three short sentences. Let your voice get quieter, not louder, as the feeling grows. Don’t rush the final line — let it land.
3
Record and Submit
When you feel ready, record yourself reading the passage with your best accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Upload your recording to Canvas.
✅ Fluency Self-Check
🎯 Did I say each word correctly, including dignity and mussed?
🌊 Did my reading sound smooth — steady and flowing between the dialogue and the narration?
🎭 Did my voice show the feeling — Johnny’s playful toughness, then the quiet, held-back emotion at the end?
📤 How to Submit

Record yourself reading the passage aloud. Upload your recording to Canvas using the assignment submission area. This is the last time you’ll read Johnny’s story aloud this way — make it count!
