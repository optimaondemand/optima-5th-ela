This Week at a Glance

Sickness spreads through the swamp camp, and Isaac’s family leaves. They follow a long line of Choctaw walkers and soldiers on a frozen road, then join a family they meet there: Gabe, Ruth, and their five-year-old daughter, Nita, who asks Isaac to be her big brother. The ghost of Mister Jonah tells Isaac the new blankets are safe and shows him the ghost walkers, the people who died and still walk beside the living. On the coldest day of the walk, Isaac’s feet freeze to the ground. His father’s words bring him back. In Writer’s Workshop, your student writes a scene where the tension keeps rising.

💛 A heads-up for families
These are some of the hardest chapters in the book. In Chapter 8, people in the swamp camp fall sick; in the novel, the blankets the soldiers handed out carried smallpox. (On the real walk, people suffered from cold, hunger and sickness; the smallpox blankets are part of Tim Tingle’s story.) In Chapter 10, Isaac stands in the ice so long that the skin of his feet tears away, and he leaves bloody footprints in the snow. In Chapter 11, Nita gives Isaac her blanket shoes. That night she rolls out of her blanket, and in the morning she has died in the snow. The women burn cedar bark and sing the Choctaw death song, and one soldier says something cruel. The lessons name these moments plainly and do not dwell on them. Reading together, or checking in after Chapters 10 and 11, helps. The unit guide has the history and what comes later.
Reading skill
How characterization builds a perspective. Your student watches what each person does, what they carry, and what they have lost, and compares how two people see the same moment on the road.
Big idea
What a person holds on to shows how they see the world. Isaac holds on to his family and his promise to Nita. His father holds on to one piece of advice.

“You cannot keep your eyes on the bloody footprints you have left behind you. You must keep your eyes on where you are going.”Isaac’s father, Chapter 10

Words to Know

Use them at home!
smallpoxa deadly disease that spreads from person to person; in the story, blankets carry it
belongingsthe things a person owns and carries with them
sicknessbeing ill; disease
refusedsaid no firmly, even when told to do something
vanisheddisappeared suddenly and completely
Word parts
anim means “life, spirit.” spir means “breathe.” Long ago, breath and life meant almost the same thing.animal · animate · unanimous · inspire · respire · expire · spirit
Spelling
-ive, -al and -ic turn a word into a describing word. Sometimes a silent e drops; sometimes the base changes more.protect → protective · season → seasonal · hero → heroic · create → creative
Grammar
Strong verbs and active voice. In active voice the subject does the action, so the reader knows who did it. Passive voice can hide that.The soldiers burned the houses. (active) · The houses were burned. (passive)

New This Week

👣 Footprints Forward
The week’s word game. Each footprint in the snow holds a word, and your student picks the ending (-ive, -al or -ic) that turns it into a describing word. Six new words a day.
🏮 Virtue Watch: perseverance
After reading, your student sorts moments, keeps a log, rates a moment on a meter, and on Day 4 picks the strongest moment of perseverance in the week.
🖼️ Winter paintings
Each lesson opens with a painting from the 1800s: a winter road, a spruce forest at sunset, a snowy forest, a lone pine. They were painted far from Mississippi, and the captions say so.
🗣️ Choctaw words
A card on Day 2 explains Halito (hello), Yakoke (thank you) and Pashofa (corn soup). The narrator never says Choctaw words aloud.

Your Student’s Writing: Rising Action

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

One short scene about conflict, start to finish this week. Day 1: note a moment, from the novel or your own life, when danger and hope existed at the same time. Day 2: write 3–5 sentences about the conflict and let the tension build, without solving it. Day 3: reread with a checklist (Is the conflict clear? Does the tension build? Is it still unresolved?), then add one sentence where everything is about to break. Day 4: write the final scene in 5–7 sentences, ending while the tension is still high, plus one sentence on what Isaac taught you about rising action.

Every day also has a Read Like a Writer box with one of Isaac’s sentences. This week they show first-person voice: a thought, a detail only Isaac could feel, and his reaction.

Poetry Corner: The Speaker’s Perspective

I’ve known rivers:
I’ve known rivers ancient as the world and older than the flow of human blood in human veins.

My soul has grown deep like the rivers.Langston Hughes, “The Negro Speaks of Rivers” (1921), opening lines, Day 2. On Day 4: Langston Hughes, “Mother to Son” (1922).

Both poems have a first-person speaker, a voice saying “I,” who is not the poet. Hughes’s river speaker has lived for thousands of years and speaks for a whole people. The speaker of “Mother to Son” is a mother. It’s the same idea as the novel: Isaac tells the story, but Tim Tingle wrote it.

How to help: Read “Mother to Son” aloud together. Ask what the mother tells her son to do, and how that sounds like what Isaac’s father says in Chapter 10.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did Isaac’s family wait before taking blankets on the walk?Listen for: they remembered the swamp blankets carried smallpox. Mister Jonah’s ghost told Isaac the new blankets were safe, so his father asked for them in the morning.
  2. What did Isaac promise Nita, and how did he keep it?Listen for: he promised to take care of her as her big brother. The next morning he cut two pieces from his own blanket and tied them on her feet as shoes.
  3. Who are the ghost walkers?Listen for: Choctaws who died from fire, sickness and hunger and still walk beside the living. Mister Jonah says, “Our lives are over, but we can still help the living.”
  4. What did Isaac’s father tell him in the snow?Listen for: not to keep his eyes on the bloody footprints behind him, but on where he is going. Isaac stopped looking back, and three days later his feet healed.
  5. What did Nita give Isaac, and what did he do?Listen for: her blanket shoes. He took them, because he had learned never to refuse a gift, and cut her a new pair.
  6. Read me your scene. Where does the tension climb?Listen for: a moment that gets more and more tense and is not solved at the end.
