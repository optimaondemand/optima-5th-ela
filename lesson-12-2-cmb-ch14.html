What Good Readers Do
Notice how setting shapes the meaning of an event

Chapter 14 brings Nat back to Salem. The setting shifts from the open sea to the harbor, from isolation to community — and everything changes. Good readers notice how the same character can act very differently depending on where he is and who is watching. As you read, ask: does Nat behave differently now that he is back on land?

🧠 Watch for this

How does the setting of Salem — society, reputation, community — shape what Nat does with his discovery about Moore’s Navigator? Is conflict still present here, or does it shift?

What does “Nineteen Guns” signify? What is being honored, and why does that matter to the story?

How does Nat’s relationship with Elizabeth (Polly) Ingersoll develop in this chapter? How does this personal element affect his character?

Poetry Corner
A poem about small things holding enormous meaning

Alfred, Lord Tennyson wrote this poem in 1869. It is very short — only six lines — but Tennyson said it captured his life’s philosophical question: what is the connection between the smallest particular and the whole universe? Read it today, as Nat returns to Salem having discovered something enormous in the smallest errors.

📜 Poetry Corner
“Flower in the Crannied Wall”
Alfred, Lord Tennyson, 1869 — public domain
Flower in the crannied wall,
I pluck you out of the crannies,
I hold you here, root and all, in my hand,
Little flower — but if I could understand
What you are, root and all, and all in all,
I should know what God and man is.
💡

Tennyson is saying: if I could fully understand even this tiny flower — its roots, its chemistry, its place in the universe — I would understand everything. This is the same logic Nat applies: if you understand the mathematics of the moon’s position precisely enough, you can know where you are anywhere on earth.

Show What You Know — prepositional phrases

“Nat worked on his corrections throughout the night.” — How many prepositional phrases? One: “on his corrections” Two: “on his corrections” AND “throughout the night”

🔤 Spelling: Identify the Correct -ous Spelling
Identify — -ous, -ious, or -eous?

Yesterday you discovered the three spellings. Today, identify the correct one for each word. Think about the base word and the pattern.

The word courag_ous: which ending is correct? -ous → couragous (drops the e) -eous → courageous (keep e to preserve soft /j/ in g)
The word ambit_ous: which ending? -ous → ambitous -ious → ambitious (base ends in a soft sound, uses -ious)

The Henry returns; the setting shifts from sea to shore

The Henry arrives in Salem Harbor. Nineteen guns — the formal salute for a returning captain — boom across the water. Nat has changed during this voyage. Now pay attention to how the setting of Salem society creates a new kind of conflict for him: how does a former indentured apprentice, now a mathematical genius, navigate a world where reputation and class still matter?

👀 Watch How — Meet Alex, our example writer

Alex builds the evidence for Body 2 (making navigation accessible).

BEFORE
Alex’s notes: Moore’s errors — hundreds of them. Sailors dying because of bad math. Nat corrects them all.
AFTER
Alex’s F+E+S: Fact: In Chapter 13, while working as supercargo on the Henry, Bowditch discovered that Moore’s Navigator — the book all sailors trusted — contained hundreds of erroneous calculations that could lead ships onto reefs. Explanation: He did not report this to someone else or write it in a complaint; he corrected every single one himself, eventually amassing over 8,000 corrections. Significance: This was not self-promotion — it was a gift to every sailor who would ever navigate by those tables, most of whom would never know his name.

Notice: Alex’s significance sentence is the most important one. It answers the reader’s unspoken question: “So what?” The answer connects directly back to the claim (knowledge accessible to everyone).
