From Boston to the Long Wharf

You finished By the Great Horn Spoon! Jack and Praiseworthy sailed 15,000 miles, dug for gold, and ended up somewhere they never planned. Now make a four-page booklet that shows how Sid Fleischman built this adventure: its shape, its speed, its jokes, and its big idea. The last page holds a piece of your own adventure.

What to do
  1. Fill in the four pages, one at a time. Keep your book open.
  2. Give a chapter number for every moment you name.
  3. Tap Make my booklet, save it as a PDF, and upload the PDF in Canvas.

Your work saves on this computer as you type. This project is about the novel. Keep real history out of it unless you say where the fact comes from.

Page 2 · Storyboard and pacing

Pick six turning points, in order, from the start of the book to the end. For each one, say whether Fleischman tells it fast or slow, and how you can tell.

Clues to pacing: short sentences and quick action feel fast; long sentences, lots of detail and characters thinking feel slow. A jump like “It was about a week before…” skips time fast.

Page 3 · Humor and pictures in words

This book makes you laugh, even in danger. Show one funny moment, then one place where Fleischman paints a picture with words.

Is it a misunderstanding, an exaggeration (like a tall tale that grows), a surprise, or the gap between how proper Praiseworthy is and how wild the gold fields are?

Look for a simile (it uses like or as), a metaphor, or a thing that acts like a person.
