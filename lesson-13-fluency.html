This week Nat looked at the moon as both scientist and wondering human being. Today’s passage is from Chapter 17 — the moonlight passage. Read it as if you can feel the night air and see the moon moving across the sky with mathematical precision.

📜 Week 13 Fluency Passage
He stood on the deck and raised his sextant to the moon. It moved across the sky the way everything moved — in a great arc, following the laws that governed the turning of the world. He measured its angle from a known star. He checked his watch. He wrote the numbers. In thirty minutes, he would measure again. And in the interval between these two observations, the moon would have moved exactly as far as the mathematics said it should. It never failed. The moon was patient, and precise, and perfectly trustworthy. So was the mathematics.
— Jean Lee Latham, Carry On, Mr. Bowditch, Chapter 17 (adapted for fluency)
🧭 Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
The first three sentences are short and measured — like the observations themselves. Let them breathe. The longer sentences that follow should flow without rushing.
2
Read for Expression
The final two sentences are parallel and conclusive — “So was the mathematics” should feel like a quiet, confident period at the end of a proof. Certainty without drama.
