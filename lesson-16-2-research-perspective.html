What Good Readers Do
Set your reading lens

Good readers of nonfiction notice the difference between a fact and a perspective. A fact can be checked and confirmed. A perspective is how someone thinks or feels about that fact.

As you read today, pay attention to which parts of the passage are stated facts and which parts are someone's reflection or point of view. Ask yourself:

🧠 Watch for this

Which sentences describe something that happened?

Which sentences describe how someone feels or thinks about what happened?

Why might two true accounts of the same event sound so different?

📜 Research Passage

By the 1830s, the United States government and many American settlers wanted more land for farming, especially for growing cotton. President Andrew Jackson supported the Indian Removal Act of 1830, a law that allowed the government to make treaties exchanging Native homelands in the Southeast for land west of the Mississippi River. Supporters of removal argued that moving the Choctaw Nation and other Native nations west would end disputes over land and open new territory for settlers.

The Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma remembers this history differently today. In its own words, the Choctaw Nation describes the Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek as a moment that "forever changed our Chahta people's story."

Both of these ideas are part of the historical record. One explains a policy decision made by a government. The other is a nation's own reflection on what that decision meant for its people. Reading both helps you understand that the same event can be described very differently, depending on who is remembering it.
