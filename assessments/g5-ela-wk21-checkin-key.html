Reading passage (Chapter 17)

Chi pisa lachike. Choctaws never say “good-bye.” There is no word for it. We say “chi pisa lachike,” which means, “I will see you again, in the future.”

Tim Tingle, How I Became a Ghost, Chapter 17

10 Reading · Written (teacher-graded) · 1 pt

What does chi pisa lachike mean? Why does it matter that Isaac and his family say it in Chapters 17 and 18?

Key: “I will see you again, in the future.” It is the last time Isaac sees his family while he is alive, but he will never leave them: “Choctaws never go away.” His mother whispers it in Ch. 18 just before he dies.

Taught in: 21.2 Reading (Ch. 17); 21.3 (Ch. 18)
Audit: kept (key checked against the book)
