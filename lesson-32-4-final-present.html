📌 Final Grammar Checklist — For Your Portfolio Piece

Before you submit your final portfolio reflection, check every sentence for:

✓ Punctuation: commas in compound/complex sentences · dialogue · apostrophes · semicolons · em dashes

✓ Capitalization: proper nouns · titles · first words of sentences and quotations

✓ Clarity: no run-ons · no dangling modifiers · consistent verb tense

Year-End Synthesis: What Reading Made of You
The questions worth answering honestly — one last time
📚 The Year in Review: Five Novels

The Witch of Blackbird Pond (Wks 1–6): Kit Tyler learns that belonging requires risk — and that the most dangerous communities are the ones that fear difference.

Johnny Tremain (Wks 7–8): A boy with a burned hand discovers that revolution requires something more than anger — it requires conviction about what you’re willing to lose.

Carry On, Mr. Bowditch (Wks 9–14): A self-taught sailor proves that the only map worth making is the one no one else has charted yet.

By the Great Horn Spoon! (Wks 25–30): A butler and a boy cross 15,000 miles and discover that courage is quiet, resourcefulness beats a map, and California is better than Boston.

Cross-Text Comparison (Wk 31): All four novels, one question: what does it mean to keep going when going is hard?

🌟 Year-End Synthesis Question 1
Which of the year’s novels surprised you most — not in plot, but in what it made you feel or think or question? Describe the surprise in 3–4 sentences. Be specific: what did you expect, what actually happened inside you, and why does that gap matter?
📓
🌟 Year-End Synthesis Question 2
If you could recommend ONE book from this year to a future 5th grader — someone just starting the journey you’re finishing — which would you choose and why? Your recommendation should be honest, specific, and include at least one thing you loved AND one thing that was genuinely hard.
📓
🌟 Year-End Synthesis Question 3 — The Final Question
Complete this sentence in 4–6 sentences — expand it into the last reflection you’ll write in 5th Grade ELA: “A reader who finishes a great book is not the same person who opened it. This year, the book that changed me most was ___, because...” End with the one sentence about reading that you want to be true for the rest of your life.
