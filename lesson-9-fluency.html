Carry On, Mr. Bowditch
🎙️ Week 9 · Fluency

This week you have been reading about Nathaniel Bowditch — a boy who holds onto mathematics when everything else slips away. Today you will practice reading a passage from Chapter 3 with accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Great readers don’t just say the words — they bring the story to life with their voice.

This Week’s Fluency Passage
Dr. Stearns did not say anything right away. When he spoke, his voice was steady and quiet. “Two plus two is four,” he said. “Always. Nobody’s going to shake you when you say that.” He put his hand on Nat’s shoulder for a moment. Then he turned and walked away down the path, and Nat stood very still in the yard.
— Jean Lee Latham, Carry On, Mr. Bowditch, Chapter 3
🗺️ Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
Read the passage again. This time, keep your voice flowing. Don’t stop between words — let the sentences move steadily, pausing only at commas and periods.
2
Read for Expression
Read it one more time. Dr. Stearns is saying something that matters — let your voice show that weight. Pause after “Always.” Read the last two sentences slowly, the way Nat might be quietly holding the words inside him.
3
Record and Submit
When you feel ready, record yourself reading the passage with your best accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Upload your recording to Canvas.
✅ Fluency Self-Check
🎯 Did I say each word correctly, including arithmetic?
🌊 Did my reading sound smooth — steady and flowing, not choppy?
🎭 Did my voice show the feeling — Dr. Stearns’ quiet certainty, Nat’s stillness?
📤 How to Submit

Record yourself reading the passage aloud. Upload your recording to Canvas using the assignment submission area. You’ve got this!
