This week you met Jack and Praiseworthy emerging from their potato barrels after two freezing days at sea. Today you will practice reading the scene of their great reveal with accuracy, smoothness, and expression. A good reader doesn’t just say the words — they give voice to Praiseworthy’s impossible dignity and Jack’s delight at being alive and free.

📜 Week 25 Fluency Passage — Chapter 1
On the second day at sea, just after dawn, the lid rose silently off a potato barrel. Cautiously, a man raised his eyes above the rim of the barrel to look about. Slowly, he unfolded his long arms and legs. Then he stood, an elegant gentleman in a black broadcloth coat. He would be the first to admit that being folded up in a barrel, with a bowler hat balanced on his knees, was not the most comfortable way to travel. Now he brushed off the hat and placed it smartly on his head. He hooked a black umbrella on his arm, for he never traveled without it, and pulled on a pair of spotless white gloves. He felt very nearly frozen solid, but permitted himself a most contented smile. Then he gave a small tap to the barrel beside him. “All clear, Master Jack.”
— Sid Fleischman, By the Great Horn Spoon!, Chapter 1
🪙 Your Fluency Path
1
Read for Smoothness
Read the passage again at a comfortable pace. Keep your voice flowing — let the long sentence about “being folded up in a barrel” unfold slowly, the way Praiseworthy himself unfolds. Don’t rush the comedy; let it land.
2
Read for Expression
Read it one more time with full expression. Praiseworthy is frozen solid but “permits himself a most contented smile.” Let your voice be formal, dignified, just slightly amused. When you reach “All clear, Master Jack” — make it sound like he’s opening the door to a grand estate, not climbing out of a vegetable barrel on a cold ship.
3
Record and Submit
When you feel ready, record yourself reading the passage with your best accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Upload your recording to Canvas.
✅ Fluency Self-Check
🎯Did I say every word correctly, including the longer words like cautiously and broadcloth?
🌊Did my reading sound smooth — like a story unfolding, not a list of words?
🎭Did my voice capture Praiseworthy: formal, unruffled, and just quietly pleased with himself?
🪙Did “All clear, Master Jack” land with the right tone — dignified, warm, perfectly timed?
📤 How to Submit

Record yourself reading the passage aloud. Upload your recording to Canvas using the assignment submission area. Read it like Praiseworthy would — with perfect composure and a contented smile.
