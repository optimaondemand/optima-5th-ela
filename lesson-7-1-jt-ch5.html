🌱 Morphology: Roots dict & scrib/script
Define · dict = say/declare · scrib/script = write

Two Latin roots appear in many English words. Dict means “say” or “declare.” Scrib and script mean “write.” Knowing these roots helps you unlock the meaning of unfamiliar words.

📌 The Core Meaning

The prefix con- and com- come from Latin and mean “together” or “with.”

Use com- before words starting with b, m, or p. Use con- everywhere else.

connect — to join things together

compose — to put parts together to make a whole

conflict — a clash or struggle between opposing forces

convene — to come together for a meeting

compress — to press or squeeze things together

From Chapter 5: The Boston Observers convened in secret at the printing shop—their shared purpose connecting them even as conflict with Britain grew.

🔤 Vocabulary
Words from Chapter 5 · Decode & Discover

When you meet a word you don’t know, use these three steps in order:

📌 Let’s try it together: “seditious”
1
Read the Sentence
Find the word in the book. What clues does the sentence give you?
“Lorne, commanding him and the other printers of Boston to quit their seditious, rebellious publications—or else.”
Mr. Lorne is a printer. The British are ordering him to stop printing things that are seditious. They pair it with the word “rebellious.” So seditious probably means something that encourages rebellion or challenges authority.
2
Look at the Word Parts
Do you see a root, prefix, or suffix you recognize?
se- + dit + -ious
The root sed- comes from the Latin seditio meaning “going apart” or “revolt.” The suffix -ious means “full of.” So seditious = full of revolt. That confirms what we guessed from the sentence.
3
Confirm
Were we close?
✅ seditious means: likely to stir up rebellion or resistance against the government or ruler in power. Our context and word parts got us there!

✏️ Grammar: Irregular Plural Nouns
Define · When plurals change more than just an ending

Most nouns form their plural by adding -s or -es. But some nouns are irregular—their plural form changes the word completely, or doesn't change at all.

👉 Common Irregular Plurals

man → men  |  woman → women  |  child → children

ox → oxen  |  tooth → teeth  |  goose → geese

deer → deer (no change!)  |  sheep → sheep (no change!)

From Chapter 5: “The men of Boston came and went”—not “the mans.” Irregular plurals must be memorized; they do not follow a rule.

🎯

Practice: Irregular Plural Identifier! Tap the word that uses the correct plural form.

The childs watched as the children ran to the harbor.

The oxen pulled the heavy carts away from the closed port.

🔠 Spelling: dict & scrib/script Roots
Define · Build word families from Latin roots

Both dict and scrib/script are Latin roots that appear throughout academic vocabulary. Learning the root helps you figure out the meaning of words you have never seen before.

📌 Build the Word Families

dict (say, declare): dictate • predict • verdict • contradict • edict

scrib / script (write): inscribe • describe • prescribe • manuscript • scripture • description

Think: When you dictate, you say words. When you inscribe, you write words. The root carries the meaning.

Argument Writing — Day 2: What Are Reasons?
★ What Is a Claim? → ○ Reasons → ○ Evidence → ○ Draft → ○ Revise → ○ Publish

🎯 Today’s focus: Learn what a CLAIM is and write one about Johnny.

👀 Watch How — Alex

Alex is writing about Johnny Tremain. Alex starts by making an observation, then turns it into a claim.

Observation (summary)

“Johnny is a good silversmith who bosses around the other apprentices.”

✗ Just says what happens. No argument.

Claim (argument)

“Johnny’s pride in his talent is actually his biggest weakness.”

✓ Takes a position. Someone could disagree.

A claim is not just what happens—it’s what you think it means. A good claim takes a position that a reader could agree or disagree with.

📋 Today’s Tool: Claim vs. Summary

Ask: Could someone disagree with this? If yes, it’s a claim. If no, it’s just a summary.

“Johnny works in a silversmith’s shop.” → ✗ Summary. No argument here.

“In Chapter 5, Johnny shows that talent without humility leads to trouble.” → ✓ Claim. Someone could disagree.

Your claim starter: “In Chapter 5, [character] shows that ___.”
