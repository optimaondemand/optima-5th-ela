This Week at a Glance

Nani Humma catches Isaac and Joseph talking by the creek, and the Choctaw Council decides to help rescue Naomi in secret. Isaac and Joseph walk back along the line to visit Isaac’s family for one last day. On the way back, a wolf attacks Isaac, and he becomes a ghost. Joseph, as the panther, drives the wolf away, and Isaac’s family carries his body on the walk. As a ghost, Isaac learns the soldiers cannot hurt him. He walks into their camp, finds Naomi tied in a wagon, and whispers her name. In Writer’s Workshop, your student revises an earlier story to make its pictures stronger.

💛 A heads-up for families
Chapter 18 is the chapter the whole book has been leading to: Isaac dies. A wolf attacks him in the woods, and the chapter describes the bite, the blood, and his family’s grief as they carry his body. Isaac tells it calmly, as a ghost who is not afraid, and he keeps helping the people he loves. In Chapter 19, Naomi is tied up at night in the soldiers’ wagon. The lessons name these moments plainly and do not dwell on them. Reading Chapter 18 together, or checking in afterward, can help.
Reading skill
How a theme grows. Your student tracks pictures and ideas that come back in the book, like the bloody footprints from Chapter 10, and explains what they mean when they return.
Big idea
The people we love never really leave us. Choctaws say chi pisa lachike, “I will see you again, in the future,” instead of good-bye.

“Choctaws never go away.”Isaac, Chapter 17

Word parts
vis / vid means “see.” spect means “look.” Evidence is what can be seen; a spectator looks on.vision · visible · invisible · evidence · spectator · inspect · perspective
Spelling
Words that sound alike. A spell checker won’t catch them, so meaning decides the spelling.their / there / they’re · its / it’s · council / counsel · affect / effect · principal / principle
Grammar
Compound and complex sentences. A compound sentence joins two ideas that could each stand alone. A complex sentence makes one idea depend on the other.When Luke returned, the bowl was empty. (complex)

New This Week

🔭 The Spyglass
The week’s word game. A brass spyglass shows one word at a time. Your student taps the root inside it (vis or spect), watches it light up, then taps what the word means. Five words a day.
🦁 Virtue Watch: courage
After reading, your student sorts moments, keeps a log, rates a moment on a meter, and on Day 4 picks the strongest moment of courage in the week.
🖼️ Paintings from the 1800s
Each lesson opens with an American painting: a deep forest, a red sunset over a river, a quiet river pool, and a moonlit night. They were painted far from Mississippi, and the captions say so.
🗣️ Choctaw words
A card on Day 2 explains Yakoke (thank you), Pashofa (corn soup) and chi pisa lachike (I will see you again). The narrator never says Choctaw words aloud.

Your Student’s Writing: Imagery and Description

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

Your student goes back to a story written in week 18 or 19 and makes its pictures stronger. Day 1: choose three moments that need stronger imagery. Day 2: revise the first moment with one detail you can see, hear, smell or feel, and one comparison. Day 3: check the first moment with a short checklist, then revise the second with a comparison that fits its feeling. Day 4: write the final revised story in 5 to 7 sentences, plus one sentence on what imagery adds.

Every day also has a Read Like a Writer box with one of Isaac’s sentences. This week they show how to explain a theme with details from more than one part of the book.

🪶

Poetry Corner: Pictures That Come Back

Who has seen the wind?
Neither I nor you:
But when the leaves hang trembling
The wind is passing thro’.Christina Rossetti, “Who Has Seen the Wind?” (1872), first stanza, Day 4. On Day 2: Langston Hughes, “Dreams” (1923).

Both poems repeat something. Hughes opens both stanzas with “Hold fast to dreams,” and each time a new picture follows. Rossetti asks the same question twice and answers it with two pictures of the wind at work. That is how a theme grows: the same idea comes back and means more.

How to help: Read the Rossetti poem aloud together. Ask how you can know something is there when you can’t see it, and how that fits Isaac and Joseph after Chapter 18.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why doesn’t the council just go to the soldiers and demand Naomi back?Listen for: the soldiers would lie, Naomi would be too afraid to tell the truth, and she might disappear. The council helps in secret instead.
  2. What do Choctaws say instead of good-bye, and what does it mean?Listen for: chi pisa lachike, “I will see you again, in the future.”
  3. What did Luke give Joseph, and why?Listen for: his blowgun. He told Joseph to take care of Isaac.
  4. What does Isaac almost do after he becomes a ghost, and what stops him?Listen for: he wants to go back into his body to end his family’s grief. He remembers his father’s words about keeping his eyes on where he is going.
  5. What did Isaac figure out in the soldiers’ camp?Listen for: the soldiers can’t hurt him because he is already a ghost, and the young man serving stew is really Naomi.
  6. Read me your revised story. Which picture is stronger now?Listen for: a detail you can see, hear, smell or feel, and a comparison.
