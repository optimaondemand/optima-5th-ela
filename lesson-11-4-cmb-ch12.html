the stars, along with the chronometer, gives nat the information he needs to find the ship's position

Corrected: The stars, along with the chronometer, give Nat the information he needs to find the ship's position.

Fixes: capitalize “The”; subject is “stars” (plural — “along with the chronometer” is a phrase) → “give.” Possessive apostrophe: “ship's.”

Poetry Corner
A poem about striving — and never stopping

Alfred, Lord Tennyson wrote “Ulysses” about the ancient hero who couldn’t stop exploring. Its final lines have echoed through centuries as a call to those who never stop pushing. Read them today, at the end of Nat’s first voyage week.

📜 Week Closing Poem
from “Ulysses”
Alfred, Lord Tennyson, 1842 — public domain
Tho’ much is taken, much abides; and tho’
We are not now that strength which in old days
Moved earth and heaven, that which we are, we are —
One equal temper of heroic hearts,
Made weak by time and fate, but strong in will
To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.
💡

Abides means remains or persists. Temper here means character or spirit. The final line — “to strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield” — uses parallelism: four infinitives in a row, building to the final refusal.

✏️ Grammar: Relative Pronouns & Clauses
Explain the importance

Last day. Now explain what the commas are really deciding. The men who could not read learned anyway means only some of the men. The men, who could not read, learned anyway means all of them could not read. Two commas, two different crews.

What Good Readers Do
Synthesize the week — name the theme

Now put the week together. The chapter titles across Chapters 9–12 make a sentence of their own: Anchor to Windward, Freedom, What Next?, Down to the Sea. Say in one full sentence what this book argues about perseverance — not the word, the claim.

🔍 How to Do It
  1. Find the moment quitting was an option: Perseverance only shows where giving up was genuinely available.
  2. Ask what keeps him going: Look for the reason the text gives, not the one you would supply yourself.
  3. Name the theme as a sentence: Say what the book argues about people who keep going. “Perseverance” is a topic; a theme is a full sentence.
🎯

Whole-book thinking: State the theme of this book about perseverance as one full sentence about people. Then find the chapter from 1–12 that gives you the strongest evidence for it, and the one that complicates it.

BEFORE
Alex’s attempt: So that’s why Nat was extraordinary. (Too weak — just says it, doesn’t show it.)
AFTER
Alex’s revised closing: Together, these examples show that Bowditch’s self-education was not passive accumulation of facts — it was deliberate, disciplined, and entirely self-directed, exactly the kind of character that would later transform navigation for thousands of sailors he would never meet.

Notice: The closing sentence doesn’t just summarize — it interprets. It tells the reader WHAT the evidence means and connects back to the claim without just repeating it.
