This Week at a Glance

The Lady Wilma sails south toward the bottom of South America. Jack hides his pet pig from the cook, and at Rio de Janeiro Praiseworthy offers him a ticket home before the dangerous waters ahead. Jack says no. Then come thirty-seven days of storms and a surprise: Captain Swain has secretly taken the ship through the Strait of Magellan. On the Pacific side the ship sits becalmed, and Jack thinks of a way to save a Frenchman’s grape cuttings with a Yankee trader’s spoiled potatoes. Day 4 looks back at how Fleischman makes the story funny.

💛 A note for families
These chapters are gentle and funny. One small sad moment: Jack’s pig is carried off when the thief escapes at Rio (the pig is safe from the cook). The places are real: Rio de Janeiro, Patagonia, the Strait of Magellan and Tierra del Fuego. A short card on Day 2 keeps real history apart from the story.
Reading skill
Humor, tone and characterization. Your student notices who a joke is on, how calm the telling stays in a storm, and what a funny moment shows about a character.
Big idea
Self-control. Praiseworthy calls the scary sea stories “Stuff and nonsense,” even though he believes every word of them.

Roots
rid / ris mean laugh and lud / lus mean play.ridiculous · derision · ludicrous · illusion
Spelling
Dialogue tags, and speech spellings where an apostrophe takes the place of a missing letter.bellowed · muttered · stammered · eatin’ = eating
Grammar
Dialogue punctuation and paragraphing: commas inside the quotation marks, and a new paragraph for each new speaker.“Pig?” said Praiseworthy. “What pig?”

New This Week

🧭 Through the Strait
The week’s word game. The ship works west through a map of the Strait of Magellan. At each narrows your student picks the channel with the right word (a real laugh or play root, or a speech spelling with the apostrophe in the right place). Each narrows passed lights a fire on the shore of Tierra del Fuego.
🌿 Virtue Watch: self-control
After reading, your student sorts everyday examples, picks the calmest moment in the storms, rates Jack’s self-control when Praiseworthy gives his idea away, and on Day 4 picks the moment of most self-control in the week.
🗺️ Real history, kept apart
A short card on Day 2: the real Strait of Magellan (about 350 miles long, first sailed by Europeans in 1520), how Tierra del Fuego got its name from the fires of the people who lived there, and a real steamship, the SS California, that took the same route in 1848–49.
📖 Read Like a Writer, every day
One line from the book a day, chosen to show voice, dialogue and reaction, like “You mean to say, sir,” said Mr. Azariah Jones, turning white, “that we’ve been bouncing around in that awful place?”

Your Student’s Writing: Drafting the Adventure Scene

Writer’s Workshop
Day 1 · PlanDay 2 · DraftDay 3 · ReviseDay 4 · Submit

Last week’s plan becomes a scene. Day 1: jot who is there, where it happens, what goes wrong and one line the hero says, then start in the middle of the action. Day 2: keep drafting, with sentence frames for help. Day 3: revise with a checklist (dialogue punctuated correctly, a new paragraph for each speaker, a reaction that shows a feeling, a hero who sounds like themself). Day 4: add one comic moment and submit.

Poetry Corner: Voice and Diction in a Comic Poem

’Twas brillig, and the slithy toves
  Did gyre and gimble in the wabe;
All mimsy were the borogoves,
  And the mome raths outgrabe.Lewis Carroll, “Jabberwocky” (1871)

Diction is word choice. In “Jabberwocky” Carroll invents words from sounds we know, and two of them, chortle and galumph, became real English words. On Day 4, “You Are Old, Father William” is a comic conversation in verse: each speaker has his own voice, and the father says ridiculous things with a perfectly straight face, a bit like Praiseworthy.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Praiseworthy offered Jack a ticket home at Rio. What did Jack say, and why?Listen for: he wouldn’t turn back; they were partners; Praiseworthy was checking he really wanted to go on (“You’ll do, Master Jack”).
  2. Why didn’t Jack see the fires of Tierra del Fuego?Listen for: the ship never went around Cape Horn; Captain Swain secretly took it through the Strait of Magellan.
  3. What is the real Strait of Magellan?Listen for: a narrow passage at the tip of South America, about 350 miles long; Magellan’s ships sailed it in 1520.
  4. How did spoiled potatoes save the grape cuttings?Listen for: each cutting was poked into a juicy potato to keep it alive; Jack knew they were juicy from being a stowaway.
  5. What do rid/ris and lud/lus mean? Name a word for each.Listen for: laugh (ridiculous, derision); play (ludicrous, illusion).
  6. Read me a line of dialogue from your scene. Where do the commas go?Listen for: inside the closing quotation marks, and a new paragraph for each new speaker.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Say it two ways: one person says “What pig?” calmly, then in a panic. Which is funnier, and why?
  • Laugh or play? Name a word, and let your student say if it has rid/ris, lud/lus, or neither.
  • Find the strait: trace the Strait of Magellan on a map or globe, between South America and Tierra del Fuego.
